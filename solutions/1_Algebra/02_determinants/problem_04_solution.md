
### Solution

#### Part 1: Computing the Determinant

The given matrix is an **upper triangular matrix** because all entries below the main diagonal are zero.

The determinant of a triangular matrix is equal to the product of its main diagonal entries.

Therefore,

$$
\det(A) = 2 \cdot (-3) \cdot 7
$$

$$
\det(A) = -42
$$

#### Part 2: General Rule for Triangular Matrices

For any $n \times n$ triangular matrix (upper or lower), the determinant is equal to the product of its diagonal entries.

The general formula is

$$
\det(A) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{ii}
$$

#### Part 3: Explanation Using This Example

In the given matrix, the diagonal entries are $2$, $-3$, and $7$.

Since all entries below the main diagonal are zero, the matrix is upper triangular. Therefore, its determinant can be calculated directly by multiplying the diagonal entries, without expanding the determinant.

Thus,

$$
\det(A) = 2 \cdot (-3) \cdot 7 = -42
$$

This example demonstrates that the determinant of any triangular matrix is equal to the product of its main diagonal entries.
